My Vision
Move materials design from search toward derivation: let engineers specify a target property, then derive the molecular network that produces it, the processing path that builds it, and the mechanical principles that connect them.

I plan to build a research program that combines theory and predictive modeling to close the design loop of soft materials, establishing mechanistic relationships from processing to molecular network structure to mechanical behavior. See the research loop →

Research Identity

I am a computational mechanician. I build predictive frameworks that link molecular architecture to the mechanics of soft and composite materials, making molecular architecture a design variable rather than an observation. My work draws on three disciplines:

Mechanics & Theory

Continuum and fracture mechanics, statistical physics, and scaling theory, used to explain why a structure produces a response and to test those explanations.

Molecular Simulation

Coarse-grained molecular dynamics that resolves crosslinks, entanglements, loops, and dangling ends explicitly, then reads the network as a graph.

Physics-Grounded ML

Graph neural operators, generative models, and symbolic regression that make structure–property maps fast enough to invert and simple enough to inspect.

Biography

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Civil and Environmental Engineering at Northwestern University (expected May 2027), advised by Prof. Sinan Keten and supported as a Ryan Fellow of the International Institute for Nanotechnology. My research reveals how nanoscale structure governs deformation, transport, and failure in soft and composite materials.

My dissertation shows that network topology controls mechanics across materials. In polymer-grafted nanoparticle assemblies, I explained how interdigitation and entanglement modulate nanoconfinement to set stiffness, toughness, and gas transport (Nano Letters, Macromolecules, J. Chem. Phys.). In crosslinked networks, I used graph-theoretic representations to link heterogeneous connectivity to large-deformation mechanics and fracture. In micellar hydrogels, I showed that long bridges between micelles toughen the gel and enable cooling-induced shape memory, which experimental collaborators then confirmed (Advanced Functional Materials).

To make topology a practical design variable, I developed TANGO, a graph neural operator that predicts the full nonlinear mechanical response of polymer networks directly from their molecular topology, making inverse design of network architecture possible.

At my core, I am a civil engineer. I earned my B.S. with High Distinction from the University of Nebraska–Lincoln (2022), where I was named Outstanding Senior, earned my EIT certification, designed roadway alignments as an intern at Olsson, and did undergraduate research on bio-inspired roadside safety hardware and on post-disaster digital reconnaissance. I see engineering as a way to improve society through the built environment and the materials that enable it.

I care about developing independent engineers. I teach from physical intuition toward formalism, train students to audit results rather than only produce them, founded the Aspiring Future Faculty Program in Northwestern CEE, and mentor undergraduate researchers. Teaching philosophy →
