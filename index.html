Research Vision
I envision a future where engineers specify a material's target property, then derive the molecular network that produces it, the processing path that builds it, and the mechanical principles that connect them.

My program closes the design loop of soft materials by combining theory and predictive modeling to trace how processing sets molecular network structure, and how that structure governs mechanical behavior.

About

I am a computational mechanician and Ph.D. candidate at Northwestern University (expected May 2027), advised by Prof. Sinan Keten. I combine molecular simulation, statistical physics, network science, and physics-grounded machine learning to reveal how nanoscale structure governs deformation, transport, and failure.

My dissertation shows that network topology (how chains are assembled, crosslinked, and entangled) controls emergent mechanics across polymer-grafted nanoparticles, crosslinked networks, and micellar hydrogels. I am now building the tools to invert that relationship: from a target mechanical response back to the molecular structure and processing conditions that produce it.

At my core I am a civil engineer. I see engineering as a way to improve society through the built environment and the materials that enable it, and I bring that purpose to my teaching and mentoring.
